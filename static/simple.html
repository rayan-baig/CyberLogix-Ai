What this is

CyberLogix watches the fridges and freezers a business depends on, and wakes somebody up when one starts to fail.

  1. A thermometer sits in the freezer. A small battery-powered box. It reads the temperature every few minutes and sends it to us, day and night, whether anybody is there or not.
  2. We watch the number. Every reading is checked against the range that food has to stay in. Not just too warm now — also warming up faster than it should be, which is the hour before it is a disaster.
  3. We phone a human until one answers. Text first, then an actual phone call, then the next person on the list. An alert nobody picks up is the same as no alert, so we keep going until somebody does.
  4. We keep the receipts. Every reading is saved and sealed. When a health inspector asks what the walk-in was doing last March, the answer is a record you can print, not a promise. If food spoils anyway, that record is what an insurer needs before they pay.

A walk-in freezer full of food is worth thousands of dollars. It fails quietly, overnight, on a weekend, and the first anybody knows is the smell on Monday. This is the thing that wakes somebody up on Saturday instead.
