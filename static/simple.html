What this is, and how it works

CyberLogix watches the fridges and freezers a business depends on, and wakes somebody up when one starts to fail.

  1. A sensor sits in each fridge and freezer. A wireless temperature sensor, bought from a sensor maker (we do not sell them). It reads the temperature every few minutes and sends it to us over the internet, day and night, whether anybody is there or not.
  2. We watch the number. Every reading is checked against the range that food has to stay in. A door opened for a minute is not an alarm: a fridge has to stay too warm for a while, or get far too warm, before anybody is woken.
  3. We text the people on your list. Everyone on call gets a text when a fridge stays too warm, saying which fridge and how warm.
  4. We keep the receipts. Every reading is saved and sealed, and kept for 13 months. When a health inspector asks what the walk-in was doing last March, the answer is a record you can print, not a promise. If food spoils anyway, that record is what an insurer needs before they pay.

A walk-in freezer full of food is worth thousands of dollars. It fails quietly, overnight, on a weekend, and the first anybody knows is the smell on Monday. This is the thing that wakes somebody up on Saturday instead.
