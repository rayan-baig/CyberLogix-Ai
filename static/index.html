An alert that can be slept through is not an alert.

Most monitoring stops at the notification. It sends a message into a channel and considers the job done. This one does not stop until a person has said, out loud, that they have it.

Every reading

Scored, not just recorded

Every reading is judged against the limit for that unit. A door opened for a minute is not an alarm: a fridge has to stay out of range (20 minutes for a food fridge, less for vaccines, and you can change it) or go far past its limit before anybody is woken, and then it is one alert, however many times the sensor reports it.

When it stays out

The whole roster is texted

Not one number on file. Everyone on call for that site, at once, with the asset, the reading and the site named in the first line — so it can be acted on from a lock screen.

If nobody answers

The phone rings

The escalation: a spoken call that reads out what is failing and asks the person to press 1. Until somebody does, it is not acknowledged, and the system keeps behaving as though nobody knows.

All the way through

Written down as it happens

Every reading, every alert, every acknowledgement, in a log that cannot be quietly edited afterwards. That record is the thing you are actually buying.

Set-up guides for each kind of sensor

Independent sensors

Your fridges can be hacked. Your alarm shouldn’t be.

In October 2026, attackers took over the fridge controls at hundreds of supermarkets in France and warmed them up overnight, as OCCRP reported.

More

A separate sensor

CyberLogix reads a separate wireless sensor in each fridge (one you buy from a sensor maker), not the fridge’s own controls, so it notices the warming even if those controls are taken over.

It reads, it never controls

It only reads temperatures and never controls a fridge, so it can’t be used to change one.

One alert for the whole site

When many units warm up together, one site-wide alert goes to your team at once, without waiting, whatever the hour.

It can’t stop an attack. It finds out in minutes and alerts your team, so somebody can act while the food is still cold.

Food and medicine, each with its own limits.

Thresholds, the language of the alert, the unit on the invoice and the paperwork produced afterwards are all specific to the trade. A flat monthly price per location for restaurants and pharmacies, a quote for every other trade, and never a share of what the monitoring saved.

The alert is the easy half.

What a business is really exposed to is the week afterwards: the insurer, the auditor, the regulator, the customer who wants to know what happened. That is where a monitoring bill justifies itself.

A record that cannot be edited

Every entry is chained to the one before it, so a reading cannot be removed or altered after the fact without the chain saying so. The value of a log in a dispute is exactly the difficulty of faking it.

The claim packet, already written

One button produces the document a loss adjuster asks for: the readings either side of the failure, when the alert went out, who acknowledged it and how long it took them.

Proof it was working

A signed attestation that a given sensor was reporting, and was being watched by a named person, over a given window — for the audit that happens when nothing went wrong at all.

For your insurer

Show your insurer proof that your fridges are watched 24/7.

Spoiled stock is one of the most common claims a restaurant, pharmacy or lab makes. Each month you can make a one-page report for each location that shows your fridges and freezers were being watched.

More

What the report says

The sensors watched and how much of the time they were online, every temperature alert and how fast somebody answered it, the longest gap in the data, and who gets alerted, by role.

Anyone can check it

Each report has a short code and a link. Your insurer opens the link and sees the same numbers. If the report was changed after it was made, the page says so.

You decide who sees it

Only you can make and share a report, and you can switch its link off at any time. It names no people, phone numbers or emails, and the check page shows only the headline numbers.

We are a monitoring service, not an insurer. Whether a report makes a difference to your policy is up to your insurer. Are you a broker or an insurer? Become a partner.
